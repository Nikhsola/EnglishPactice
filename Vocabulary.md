# Vocabulary

## August 20, 2026

No new vocabulary was introduced or practiced in this session.

## 2026-09-08

### Needs reinforcement

- **plan to + verb** — use for intentions or planned actions.
  - Example: “I plan to take the English exam this week.”
- **prepare for + noun** — get ready for something.
  - Example: “I plan to prepare for the assessment this week.”
  - Avoid: “prepare to the assessment.”
- **be worried about + noun** — feel concerned about something.
  - Example: “I’m worried about the results.”
- **be confident (that) + clause** — feel sure about an ability or outcome.
  - Example: “I’m not confident I can reach B2 yet.”
- **mow the lawn** — cut the grass using a lawn mower.
  - Example: “I plan to mow the lawn this week.”
- **topic** — the natural word for what a conversation or discussion is about; generally preferable to “subject” in the session-planning context.
  - Example: “It’s better to choose a topic related to my mistakes.”

## 2026-09-09

### Needs reinforcement

- **be worried that + clause** — use when the worry is about something that may happen.
  - Example: “I’m worried that I’ll get stuck on simple questions.”
  - Reinforce **I’ll get**, not “I get,” for the anticipated future situation practiced in this session.
- **get stuck on + noun** — become unable to continue with a particular question/problem.
  - Example: “I might get stuck on a simple question.”
  - Avoid: “get stuck in the simple questions.”
- **under pressure** — while experiencing stress or demands.
  - Example: “It’s harder for me to remember words under pressure.”
  - Avoid: “on the pressure.”
- **go blank / my mind goes blank** — temporarily be unable to remember or think of what to say.
  - Example: “My mind might go blank under pressure.”
- **blank vs. blink** — pronunciation/meaning distinction to recycle.
  - **blank**: empty; in “my mind goes blank,” unable to think of the answer.
  - **blink**: briefly close and open the eyes.
- **If I had…, I would…** — hypothetical pattern used for an imagined assessment tomorrow.
  - Example: “If I had the English assessment tomorrow, I would practice speaking more.”
