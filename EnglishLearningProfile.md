# English Learning Profile

_Last updated: September 9, 2026_

## 1. Learner Overview

The learner practices English through spontaneous, real-time conversation and wants to become more natural and confident in spoken English, progressing toward B2/C1 fluency.

A key feature of the learner's style is active curiosity: they frequently stop to ask what a word or phrase really means, how native speakers use it, and whether two expressions are truly synonymous.

The learner benefits strongly from repetition immediately after correction rather than simply hearing the correct version.

## 2. Preferred Coaching Style

- Correct important or recurring mistakes, but do not interrupt constantly for minor slips.
- Give the natural English version briefly.
- Ask the learner to repeat the corrected sentence.
- If the learner makes a mistake during repetition, correct it and have them try again.
- Do not move on too quickly after an important correction.
- Use realistic conversation rather than isolated grammar drills.
- Explain subtle differences between words and expressions with concrete examples.
- Recycle recently learned expressions later in conversation.
- Keep explanations conversational and relatively short.
- Prioritize natural spoken English over exhaustive grammatical terminology.
- Keep the conversation active after a correction loop is completed; the learner prefers the coach to proactively ask the next relevant question rather than waiting silently.
- When a repeated correction is not succeeding, explicitly contrast what the learner is saying with the target form rather than merely asking for another repetition. The learner needs to understand exactly where the error is.

Default learning loop: **Correction → explanation/contrast when needed → repetition → confirmation → continue**

## 4. Vocabulary and Natural English

- “I’m worried that I’ll get stuck on simple questions.”

The learner benefits from semantic contrasts. On September 9, **blank** vs. **blink** required explicit explanation: **blink** is an eye movement; **blank** in “my mind goes blank” means temporarily being unable to remember or think of what to say.

## 5. Pronunciation Notes

Pronunciation feedback must remain evidence-based. Spoken production often includes fillers, repeated words, false starts, and self-corrections.

September 9 provided strong session-level evidence of difficulty distinguishing/producing **blank** versus **blink** in the phrase “go blank.” After explicit contrast and isolated-word practice, the learner successfully produced “blank,” “go blank,” and finally the complete phrase “because my mind might go blank under pressure.” Recycle naturally before deciding whether this is a persistent pronunciation issue.

The earlier word “lawn” also required focused repetition on September 8, but evidence remains insufficient to label it a recurring pronunciation problem.

## 6. Fluency Analysis

The learner communicates successfully despite frequent hesitation. Typical characteristics include fillers, repeated words, restarting sentences, self-correction, and occasional difficulty retrieving a phrase.

On September 9, longer assessment-style answers contained many fillers and restarts, but repeated chunk practice led to a substantially cleaner final answer. A key priority is automatic retrieval of common chunks under pressure rather than simply increasing speaking speed.

## 10. Coaching Protocol for Future Sessions

Start naturally and use realistic conversation. When an important mistake appears:
1. Identify the exact problematic part.
2. Give the learner an opportunity to self-correct.
3. If unsuccessful, contrast their version with the target version and explain the difference briefly.
4. Ask for a short repetition.
5. Repeat until acceptable, but avoid mechanical repetition without explanation.
6. Immediately continue with a related task and recycle the structure later.

Near-term recycling targets:
- **I plan to + verb**
- **prepare for + noun**
- **be worried about + noun**
- **be worried that + clause**
- **get stuck on + noun**
- **under pressure**
- **my mind goes/might go blank**
- hypothetical pattern **If I had…, I would…**

## Core Principle

**Natural conversation + targeted correction + clear explanation + mandatory repetition + recycling.**
